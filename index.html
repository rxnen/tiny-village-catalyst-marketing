From Vision to Village

Tiny Village Catalyst is a free, open-source guide that provides siting, permitting, cost, and funding solutions for affordable housing projects.

Try the demo →

The Problem

1

Community groups have the will to build emergency housing for their homeless population.

2

Cities have funding and resources allocated specifically for supporting affordable housing projects.

3

Only a handful of people have knowledge and experience from past projects.

Tiny Village Catalyst is a village-building expert, available to every community at any time.

What it does

1

Ask

Type a real question in plain language >  “Can we build on church-owned land in our city?”

2

Get a grounded answer

Every answer comes from a curated library of real village experience, local codes, cost data, and funder profiles, and cites its source so you can check it.

3

Decide, with backup

For zoning, legal, and money questions, the tool flags what needs expert review and connects you with people who’ve done it.

01 Community readiness (Coming soon)

Identify the stage a coalition is in, what's unresolved, and what plans need to be made next.

02 Land identification (Live prototype)

Ingests public parcel, land use, environmental, and physical suitability data, then filters and ranks parcels for tiny village viability. Validated against tiny villages that are currently up and running.

03 Budget and funding (Coming soon)

Costed bills of materials and project-level cost data from previous tiny village projects help a coalition produce a credible budget to raise against.

04 Design and configuration (Coming soon)

How many units a site supports, which layouts have worked, and which shared facilities a village requires — drawn from the Oakland and Richmond builds.

05 Permitting and approvals (Coming soon)

Which approvals come in what order in your jurisdiction, what a shelter crisis declaration changes, and the permitting precedent from villages that got built.

Who needs it

The Catalyst is for groups that have decided to act but have no planning staff or domain knowledge to make it happen.

•Congregations •Nonprofit coalitions •Neighborhood associations •Community councils •Service providers •Small cities •Informal groups of neighbors

Residents are part of the entire planning process. The platform ensures usability by a resident leader on a coalition.

Who we are

Built by Tiny Village Spirit

A Bay Area non-profit that helps communities build tiny home villages for unhoused neighbors.

The Catalyst is our documented experience from two constituent-led builds — the Tiny House Empowerment Village in Oakland, envisioned and led by the young people who now live there, and our village in Richmond — alongside 60+ partner organizations and 3,000+ volunteers.
